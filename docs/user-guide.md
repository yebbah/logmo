# logmo: User guide

logmo helps you log your weight, food, water, steps and how you feel each day, set goals week by week, and see whether you're on track. Everything stays on your iPhone: there's no account and nothing is sent anywhere.

---

## 1. The five tabs

| Tab | What it's for |
|---|---|
| **Dashboard** | Your campaign, this week's goals, and charts of your weight, steps, calories and how you feel |
| **Streaks** | Your streaks, your compliance, and how the last days and weeks went |
| **Log** | Where you enter each day |
| **Campaigns** | Run a cut, bulk or maintenance campaign, plan its weeks, and look back at past ones |
| **Settings** | Units, themes, required trackers, reminders, cycle and sick tracking, backup |

When the app opens you'll see the logo for a moment. Tap it to skip.

## 2. Getting started

On the Dashboard a **Get set up** card walks you through three steps:
1. **Start a campaign:** choose cut, bulk or maintenance, a target weight if you have one, a start date and how many weeks.
2. **Set your weekly goals** for it.
3. **Log your first day.**

Tap **Not now** to put the card off until tomorrow.

## 3. Logging a day

Open the **Log** tab. The date at the top shows today; tap it to pick another day, or use the arrows. You can log any past day, never a future one.

- **Weight** in kg or lb (change in Settings).
- **Food:** calories, protein, carbs and fat.
- **Water** in ml, with quick buttons for +250, +500, +750 and +1,000.
- **Steps.**
- **Check-in:** seven ratings from 1 to 5: sleep, fatigue, stress, hunger, training performance, appetite and soreness. Under each you see what 1 and 5 mean. Tap a rating again to clear it. For **training performance**, tap **n/a** on a rest day.
- **Cycle** (only if you turn it on) and **Health** (only if you turn it on): see section 9.

Everything saves by itself; the top right shows **Saved**. To put the keyboard away, tap **Done** above it or drag the screen down. A day with nothing entered isn't a record.

**Still to log:** under the date, a row of icons shows the trackers you've chosen as required (scale, fork and knife, drop, walking figure, checklist). Each icon has three colours: **green** with a tick when that tracker is done and the day's goal is reached, **amber** with a ring when something is logged but it isn't complete or the goal isn't reached yet (for example water under its goal, or food without every number), and **grey** when nothing is logged. Food needs all four numbers; the check-in needs all seven ratings. When your campaign asks for body measurements, **Measurements due** appears under the icons.

Fields show a hint when you have a goal, for example "goal 150" for protein, and "guide 210" for carbs and fat.

### Body measurements

Measure with a tape, in centimetres, relaxed and at about the same time of day: **waist at the navel**, **waist 2 in (about 5 cm) below** and **2 in above** the navel, **chest**, **thighs**, **calves** and **biceps** (relaxed). Everything is optional.

- **Set how often per campaign.** When you start a campaign (or edit one that hasn't started), choose **Measurements and photos: Off, Weekly, Biweekly or Monthly** (monthly means every four weeks). Measurements are always due on the **first day** (your baseline) and again in the **last week** (your end state), with the cadence in between.
- **Progress photos.** Turn on **Ask about progress photos on those days** and the Log screen asks "Have you taken your progress photos?" on the same days. logmo records only your yes; the photos themselves stay in your Photos app.
- **On the Log screen,** a **Measurements** section opens on a due day, marked **Due today**, with your last reading beside each field. On other days it waits behind **Add measurements**, so you can add one any day. It stays due until you measure.
- **See the results** on the Dashboard and in a campaign's detailed view: a line for each site showing how it has changed since your first reading, a switch for each line, and a table with the latest reading and the change.
- The weekly suggestion reads them as a second opinion beside the scale. They explain what is happening but never change your goals on their own. Measurements are typed here only; they don't come from Apple Health.

## 4. Goals

Goals are set **per week**, and each campaign keeps its own. Open them from the **Goals** card on the Dashboard (**Edit goals**) or the **Weekly plan** (section 6).

| Goal | How it's judged |
|---|---|
| **Calories** (a daily figure, for example 1,800) | On the week's average. Eat more some days and less on others as long as the week averages out. Choose "at or under" or "at or over" |
| **Protein, water** | Met on a day at or above the goal |
| **Steps** | Either each day's total reaches the goal, or the week's average does |
| **Workout days a week** | A workout day is a day with a training performance rating. Mark rest days n/a. Judged for the week |
| **Carbs and fat** | Guidance only. They show as hints and in the plan, and are never marked met or missed |

A **weigh-in counts as a daily goal.** A day isn't complete unless you weighed in.

A week with an unlogged day can't be "met", and missing values are never treated as zero.

## 5. Streaks and compliance

The three streaks (logging, daily goals, weekly goals) are **not campaign-specific**: they count across all your campaigns. The daily goals streak needs a weigh-in and every daily goal you set; a day outside any campaign, or a week with no weekly goals or no campaign, breaks the goals streaks. A goals streak judges each day or week against the goals of the campaign it belonged to, so it carries on when you start a new campaign.

On the **Streaks** tab:
- **Streaks** count consecutive days: logging, daily goals met, and weekly goals met. Today not being logged yet doesn't break a streak.
- **Compliance** is the share of days you did everything: the trackers you require fully logged, the daily goals met (weigh-in included), and the week's goals on track. Today counts and turns green when everything for it is done.
- **Daily goals, last 7 days** and **Weekly goals** show a tick, a cross, or a hollow circle. A hollow circle is today (still open), a week still open, or a day before you started logging.

Choose which trackers count as required in **Settings → Required for a complete day**.

## 6. Campaigns and the weekly plan

The Dashboard opens with **which week you are in** ("Week 4 of 8") and this week's dates. A **Weekly rate of change** card, like your workbook's weekly line, lists each week's trend weight and how far it moved from the week before. In a campaign's first week, if its starting weight is more than 0.3 kg from where your trend was, the change shows **N/A**: the jump is the trend restarting from the weight you entered, not weight you gained or lost. The Dashboard's right end of the week line shows how many **days are left** in your running campaign. Tap it to open the Campaigns tab.

### Campaigns tab
- **Every campaign has a start and an end.** There is no automatic campaign: you start each one, with a start date and a length (maintenance too). When none covers today the Dashboard and this tab say **No campaign running** and offer **Start a campaign**. Days between campaigns belong to none, so goals, goal streaks and compliance only count days inside a campaign; logging and your charts don't need one. A new campaign can start in the past to cover earlier days you logged.
- **Running now** shows your campaign, the week and days left. Buttons: **Weekly plan**, **Rename**, **Extend** (1, 2, 4 or 8 weeks) and **End campaign**.
- **+ New** starts a campaign. It asks for a **starting weight** (filled in with your current trend weight; change it to start afresh). The trend starts from it on the campaign's first day. You can change it later on a running or upcoming campaign, but not once it has ended. It can start today, in the past, or up to a year ahead, and can have a label such as "Summer cut".
- If a campaign is running, the next one is planned to start after its last day. If you choose a later start, maintenance fills the days in between. Only one campaign can be planned at a time.
- **Look back** lists every campaign: the weight trend change, compliance, average steps and calories, and tap in for the full results and each week's goals.

### Changing campaigns
- A **running** campaign can be extended. If a planned campaign would start too soon, you're offered to move that one's start date first.
- A **planned** (upcoming) campaign can have its **start date and length** edited. Its weekly goals are copied again for the new weeks.
- A **past** campaign can't be changed at all: not its label, dates or goals.
- A week before a campaign ends, the Dashboard asks whether to extend it, or, if another is planned, whether to move that one's start date.
- When a campaign's weeks are over, a card offers **End & see results** or **Extend 2 weeks**.

### Weekly plan
Open it from the Campaigns tab or the Goals card. This week comes first, then **Plan ahead** (the next four weeks), then **Past weeks**.
- You can change this week and the next four. **Past weeks are locked**, because changing one would change its result and your streaks.
- Each week says **Set by you** or **Carried over**. New weeks start as a copy of the week before; **Same as before** confirms it and **Edit** changes it. Changes from the week before are highlighted.
- The Dashboard asks you to confirm carried-over goals, and on Sundays to plan the next week.

## 7. Suggest next week (Apple Intelligence)

Tap **Suggest** on the Goals card, the sparkles button on the Weekly plan, or the button on the plan prompts. You can do this any day.

1. **See what's shared.** A sheet lists exactly what the assistant sees: numbers only (your campaign type, weight trend, averages against goals, workouts, check-in averages, and period and sick days if you track them, each with an off switch). No name, dates or notes. Choose **This week so far** or **Last week**. The more days you've logged, the more confident it can be.
2. **Get a suggestion.** logmo works out the facts first: your weight trend over up to 3 weeks, what you actually ate and walked against your goals, how you've been following the plan, and your check-ins. It then proposes next week's calories, protein, carbs, fat, steps, lifting days and water.
3. **If Apple Intelligence is slow,** logmo waits up to 25 seconds and then shows the suggestion it calculated itself, and says so.
4. **Choose a pace.** When your data supports a faster option you see a **Conservative** and an **Aggressive** option side by side, each with its calories and the weight change it points to (tap one to compare). **Settings → Weekly suggestion → Preferred pace** decides which is selected first and which one Apple Intelligence writes about. Aggressive is only offered when your maintenance can be estimated, your logs are reliable, and nothing argues against it (illness, strained recovery, high hunger on a cut); otherwise you get one pace and the reason. Both keep the same safety limits.
4b. **Read why.** You see a short headline (keep, raise or lower calories), a **confidence** level (high, medium or low, with a breakdown for weigh-ins, calorie logs, steps and check-ins), a plain-language **Why** (what your weight, intake, check-ins and measurements say, in a few short lines; the numbers behind it are one tap away), anything worth knowing, and what more to log if the data is thin. Days you didn't log are never counted as zero.
5. **Decide.** **Use for next week** saves it as "Set by you". **Edit first** opens the week editor with it filled in. **Not now** saves nothing.

How it decides: calories change only when your weight is outside what your campaign aims for, there's enough data to trust that, the last few days agree, and you've actually been eating near your target. If you've been well off your target, it keeps the target and says following it comes first. With low confidence it keeps your targets and tells you what to log. It won't lower a calorie target that is already low, never cuts harder in a sick or strained week, and never raises steps or lifting days by itself. Check-in scores are self-reports, so it never says whether training is safe.

It runs on your iPhone, so nothing leaves it. It needs iOS 26 or later with Apple Intelligence turned on. Without that you still get a simpler, rule-based suggestion. It is a suggestion, not medical advice.

### Weekly review

On the last day of the week (Sunday) a card on the Dashboard says **Week 4 ends today**. Tap **Review my week**, or **Later** to put it off for the day. Any other day, tap **Review** on the Goals card or the review button on the Weekly plan to see the week so far. You can switch between **This week** and **Last week**.

- **At the top:** how the week went in one word (on track, mostly on track, needs attention) and a short summary. With Apple Intelligence on, the wording is polished on your iPhone using only the numbers.
- **How the week went:** weight trend (and how far from your target), calories, protein days, steps, water, training (lifting days, how training felt, and strength minutes from Apple Health), how you felt, and measurements if you took them. Each row shows last week beside it, with a green, amber or blue mark.
- **Biofeedback:** your seven ratings with their change on last week; green moved the better way.
- **Plan next week** opens the weekly suggestion. The review is worked out from your logs each time and never changes your goals. Training from other apps (for example Hevy) isn't included yet.

## 8. Reminders

In **Settings → Reminders**:
- **Weekly plan reminder:** a notification once a week at the day and time you choose: "Plan your week".
- **Daily log reminder:** a notification at the time you choose, **only on days you haven't finished your daily goals** (or, with no daily goals set, your required trackers). Log what's missing and today's reminder is cancelled. It says "Time to log".

Neither shows anything from your logs. The first time you turn one on, iOS asks for permission. If you said no, Settings shows a button to open iOS Settings.

## 9. Cycle and sick tracking

Both are optional and off by default.
- **Settings → Cycle → Track my menstrual cycle** adds "Are you on your period today?" to the Log tab. Days marked yes show as **red dots** on your weigh-in chart and trend.
- **Settings → Health → Track when I'm sick** adds "Are you sick today?". Sick days show as **yellow dots**.

Neither counts against your compliance or streaks. Turning one off hides the question and the dots but keeps what you entered.

## 10. The Dashboard

Choose what to look at with **Showing**: your current campaign, **Overall (all time)**, or any past campaign. From the top: the week you're in, **Recent performance** (with your target weight and how far you are from it), **Estimated maintenance**, **Weigh-ins and trend**, the **Weekly rate of change** as a bar graph (one bar per week), **Body measurements**, steps and calories, and how you feel (biofeedback). Tap the expand button on a chart to open it full screen in landscape.

- Change how smooth the trend line is in **Settings → Trend → Weight smoothing**.
- Turn the biofeedback lines on or off with **Show biofeedback** on the chart.

## 11. Apple Health

**Settings → Connections → Apple Health** brings in what you already track elsewhere, so you type less. logmo only **reads** from Health and never writes to it, and nothing leaves your iPhone.

1. Tap **Connect Apple Health**. iOS asks which types logmo may read, one at a time.
2. Choose what to bring in: **Weight**, **Steps**, **Water**, **Food** (calories and macros, if a nutrition app writes them to Health), **Strength workouts**, **Sleep hours**, **Cycle**, **Resting heart rate and HRV**, and **Body fat and lean mass**. Cycle starts off and only works if "Track my menstrual cycle" is on.

- **Updates:** logmo updates when it opens, whenever you come back to it, and when you open the Log screen. It reads the last week by itself, and any older day you open on the Log screen. **Import earlier days** in the Apple Health settings reads the last 30 days, 3 months or a year. When logmo starts reading a new type of data, iOS asks you once. Steps and food keep updating while the day is open. Tap **Sync** on the Log screen, or **Update now** in Settings, to do it right away.
- **Health marks:** an imported value shows a small **Health** mark beside its label on the Log screen.
- **The last action wins:** the automatic updates never overwrite what you typed, and if you change or clear an imported value, Health leaves it alone for that day. Pressing **Sync** on the Log screen is different: it replaces what you typed on the day you're viewing with Health's values. Type something afterwards and yours stands until you press Sync again. **Use Health's** under a field hands back just that field.
- **Nothing is invented:** a zero or missing reading is never imported, and a day with no Health data stays empty.
- **Strength workouts:** if Health recorded one and you haven't rated the day, logmo asks. **Rate it** takes you to the Training performance row; **Not a lifting day** marks training performance as n/a. It never rates for you.
- **Sleep** is scored for you: Health only knows how long you slept, so logmo turns that into your **Sleep rating** from 1 to 5 (it shows as a Health value you can change). 7½ to 9½ hours is a 5; 6¾ to 7½ hours a 4; 6 to 6¾ hours a 3; 4½ to 6 hours a 2; under 4½ hours a 1. If your watch records sleep stages, they count too: lots of waking, or very little deep and REM sleep, takes a point or two off, and a night with good deep and REM sleep and few wake-ups can add one (for nights of 6 hours or more). The Log screen explains the score, for example "2 h 46 min asleep: 1 of 5". A night is scored after 11:00, and very short nights are flagged in case it's only a partial recording or a nap. It is an estimate from the numbers, not a measure of how rested you feel, so change it whenever it's off.
- **Cycle** fills "on your period" with Yes on days Health has menstrual flow recorded.
- **Resting heart rate and HRV** go into the weekly suggestion as recovery context: if heart rate is up and HRV is down on the week before, it says so. They never change your targets on their own.
- **Body fat and lean mass** show under your weight on the Log screen and as a Body composition card on the Dashboard, with the change since the first reading in the period.
- **Missing something?** iOS lets you decline a type without telling logmo. Open **Health → Sharing → Apps → logmo** to check.

Imported values count for everything else exactly like typed ones: Still to log, goals, streaks, compliance, reminders and the weekly suggestion.

## 12. Estimated maintenance

The **Estimated maintenance** card on the Dashboard shows roughly how many calories hold your weight steady, worked out from your own logs the same way your workbook does: your smoothed weight, what you eat, and how fast your trend is moving. If you're losing weight while eating 2,000 a day, maintenance has to be higher than 2,000, and the card says by how much.

- **The card** shows the estimate with a status (**Updating**, **Holding** or **Formula**), one line saying how far your goal is below or above it, a chart over time, and a table by week of goal, eaten and maintenance. The note under it says which smoothed intake the estimate is built from.
- **A warning** appears if what you've logged is under 70% of your goal: some days may only be partly logged, which makes maintenance come out too low.
- **It needs data:** four weeks of logging in all, counted from your first day across every campaign (so a new campaign that carries on from an earlier one keeps your history, and doesn't start over), with a weigh-in on at least five of the last ten days and calories on at least seven. When logs are too thin it **holds** the last estimate rather than guessing, and a day you didn't log is never counted as zero.
- **About you (optional):** in Settings → About you, add your sex, age, height and activity level. Until your own logs have built an estimate, logmo shows a formula estimate from these, and afterwards it uses it as a cross-check. It stays on your iPhone and is never sent anywhere.
- **In the weekly suggestion,** maintenance is shown as evidence and used to size a calorie change and to make sure the change is going the right way. It never raises the weekly limit on how much a target can move.

It's an estimate, not a measurement, and not medical advice. If it looks wrong, the usual cause is incomplete calorie or weigh-in logging.

## 13. Backup, export and deleting

**Settings → Backup**
- **Save a full backup:** one file with all your logs, weekly goals, campaigns and settings. Keep it in Files or iCloud Drive. Do this regularly.
- **Restore from a backup:** replaces everything on the phone with the file's contents, after asking.
- **Export (CSV):** daily logs (with each day's campaign and goals), campaigns, and weekly goals, for spreadsheets. These can't be restored.

**Delete all data** (bottom of Settings) removes every log, goal and campaign and starts fresh.

## 14. Themes and units

- **Settings → Appearance → Theme:** Clean (light) or Midnight (dark).
- **Settings → Units → Weight:** kilograms or pounds. Your data is stored in kg whichever you pick.

## 15. Questions

**Is my data safe?** It's stored only on your iPhone. Nothing is uploaded and there's no account. Because of that, a lost or reset phone loses the data, so save a full backup now and then.

**The app won't open.** If you installed it yourself with a free Apple account, it stops opening after 7 days. Don't delete it: reinstall over it and your data stays. If it was deleted, restore your latest backup.

**Why is a day marked missed?** A day needs a weigh-in, the daily goals met (protein, steps, water as set), the required trackers fully logged, and the week's goals on track. Open the Log for that day to see what's missing.

**Why did my streak drop?** A day without a weigh-in or with a goal missed ends the daily-goals streak. Today not yet logged doesn't.

**Can I edit a past week's goals?** No. Past weeks and past campaigns are locked so results and streaks stay true.

**Do I need an internet connection?** No.

**How do I contact the developer?** Settings → About → Contact the developer. The email carries only the app and iOS version, nothing from your logs.
